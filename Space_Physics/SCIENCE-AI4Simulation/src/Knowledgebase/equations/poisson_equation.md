# Equation Card: Poisson Equation

## 0. Equation Identity

- **Name**: Poisson Equation
- **Type**: Elliptic Partial Differential Equation
- **Order**: Second-order (in space)
- **Linearity**: Linear
- **Time Dependence**: None (steady-state)
- **Primary Field**: Scalar field u(x)

---

## 1. Canonical Mathematical Form

### 1.1 Strong Form

$$
- \nabla \cdot \left( k(x)\, \nabla u(x) \right) = f(x), \quad x \in \Omega
$$

Special case (constant coefficient):

$$
- \Delta u(x) = f(x), \quad x \in \Omega
$$

where

- $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^d$ is a bounded spatial domain
- $u(x)$ is the unknown scalar field
- $k(x)$ is a material or medium coefficient
- $f(x)$ is a prescribed source term

---

## 2. Physical Interpretation

The Poisson equation describes **equilibrium states** where a scalar field is driven by distributed sources.

Typical interpretations include:

- Electrostatics: electric potential generated by charge density
- Heat conduction: steady temperature field with internal heat sources
- Gravitation: gravitational potential induced by mass density
- Diffusion equilibrium: steady concentration under sources/sinks

---

## 3. Variables and Parameters

| Symbol | Meaning | Typical Unit | Notes |
|------|--------|-------------|------|
| $u(x)$ | Scalar field | problem-dependent | Potential, temperature, concentration |
| $k(x)$ | Diffusion / conductivity coefficient | varies | Often positive, may be spatially varying |
| $f(x)$ | Source term | varies | External forcing or generation |
| $\Omega$ | Spatial domain | – | Open bounded domain |
| $\partial\Omega$ | Domain boundary | – | Boundary where BCs apply |

---

## 4. Source / Forcing Term

The Poisson equation **requires** an explicit source term.

Canonical source forms include:

- Zero source (Laplace equation):  
  $$
  f(x) = 0
  $$

- Constant source:  
  $$
  f(x) = f_0
  $$

- Spatially varying source:  
  $$
  f(x) = f(x)
  $$

Source terms represent **charge density, heat generation, or body forces**, depending on context.

---

## 5. Boundary Conditions (Required)

To form a well-posed problem, boundary conditions must be specified on $\partial\Omega$.

Typical admissible BCs include:

- **Dirichlet BC** (value prescribed):
$$
u(x) = g(x), \quad x \in \partial\Omega_D
$$

- **Neumann BC** (flux prescribed):
$$
k(x)\,\nabla u(x)\cdot n = q(x), \quad x \in \partial\Omega_N
$$

- **Robin BC** (mixed):
$$
\alpha u + \beta\, k(x)\,\nabla u \cdot n = r(x)
$$

At least one Dirichlet condition (or equivalent constraint) is required to ensure uniqueness.

---

## 6. Initial Conditions

- **Not required**.

The Poisson equation is a **steady-state** (time-independent) elliptic PDE.
Initial conditions must **not** be specified.

---

## 7. Well-Posedness Conditions

A Poisson problem is well-posed if:

1. $\Omega$ is bounded and sufficiently regular
2. $k(x) > 0$ almost everywhere in $\Omega$
3. Boundary conditions cover the entire $\partial\Omega$
4. For pure Neumann problems:
$$
\int_\Omega f(x)\,dx = \int_{\partial\Omega} q(x)\,ds
$$
(to ensure solvability)

---

## 8. Typical Modeling Assumptions

- Quasi-static equilibrium
- No time dependence
- Linear constitutive relation between flux and gradient
- Homogeneous or piecewise-smooth material coefficients

---

## 9. Common Pitfalls

- Forgetting to specify boundary conditions
- Mixing incompatible BC types without physical justification
- Treating Poisson equation as time-dependent
- Using pure Neumann BCs without compatibility constraints
- Confusing Poisson ($f \neq 0$) with Laplace ($f = 0$)

---

## 10. Applicability to PINN-Based Solvers

The Poisson equation is well-suited for PINNs due to:

- Simple residual form:
$$
\mathcal{R}(x) = -\nabla \cdot (k \nabla u) - f(x)
$$
- Clear separation of PDE loss and BC loss
- Wide use as benchmark for elliptic PDE solvers
